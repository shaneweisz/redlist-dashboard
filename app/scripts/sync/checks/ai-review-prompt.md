You are checking how species on the IUCN Red List are matched to the Catalogue of Life (CoL), the taxonomy GBIF files its occurrence records under.

The JSON on stdin holds a batch of Red List species. For each: the Red List's name, author citation, lineage, listed synonyms (with their authors) and, where the assessment has them, its taxonomic notes; GBIF's name matcher's response for the bare name (GBIF files records identified by the bare name under the matcher's answer); and every CoL record (a name usage) with that name, the matcher's answer's name, or a synonym's name. Each record gives its status (ACCEPTED; PROVISIONALLY_ACCEPTED, often a duplicate of an accepted record; SYNONYM; AMBIGUOUS_SYNONYM, a name CoL files under more than one species; MISAPPLIED, the name as wrongly used for another organism), the CoL species it leads to (`col_species`, null when it leads to none) with that species' GBIF records, the GBIF records under the record itself (`gbif_records_under`: the record and what CoL files beneath it; for an accepted record, its synonyms and subspecies too), whether the record's own name is another Red List species' (`name_assessed_separately`), whether its CoL species is another Red List species (`col_species_assessed_as`), which other Red List species have their own name filed under that CoL species (`col_species_holds_assessed`), and which other Red List species the rules count that CoL species for (`col_species_counted_whole_by`).

What counts is the Red List's concept of this species: what its assessment includes (its taxonomic notes and synonyms), not the newest treatment. Use sources to learn which names, and so which CoL records, belong to that concept.

For each species, say which of its `col_records` (by id, with the record's name) hold this Red List species' GBIF records, and how each relates to it. Each GBIF record counts for one Red List species at most:

- **is**: the record's CoL species is this species, perhaps under another name or spelling, so all of that CoL species' records count. Answer **is** for a record that is itself the CoL species (accepted or provisionally accepted) even when CoL's species also includes a species the Red List assesses separately: that species keeps its own records.
- **split into**: CoL holds as a separate species something lying wholly inside this one (the Red List lumps what CoL splits), so that whole CoL species counts too.
- **part of**: only the records filed under this record are this species; its CoL species is another or broader one. Use it for a synonym or misapplied record, never for a record that is itself the CoL species. A record whose `col_species` is null leads to no species: only **part of** applies.

Never answer **is** or **split into** for a CoL species that `col_species_assessed_as` or `col_species_counted_whole_by` names: that species is assessed on its own (if it is the same organism, that is a duplicate assessment, and its records stay with it). **part of** is still right for a record filed there under this species' own name, as when CoL files this species' name and author under an unrelated species by mistake. Leave out any record whose own name is another Red List species' (`name_assessed_separately`), and any MISAPPLIED record unless a source shows the records under that usage are this species. Leave out every record whose records are not this species'. An empty list means none of these records is this species and it is not in CoL; if CoL holds it under a name not listed here, answer "can't tell" and give that name in the reason.

Every species here is one the rules couldn't settle, so look each one up before you answer: search the web for its treatment in an authoritative source for its group (for example Plants of the World Online or World Flora Online for plants, the Catalog of Fishes, Amphibian Species of the World, the Reptile Database, the Mammal Diversity Database, the IOC World Bird List or Avibase, WoRMS or MolluscaBase, or the taxon's latest revision), and give the pages you read as URLs in its sources. Answer only from a source you read that states the treatment: CoL, GBIF and sites that copy them (iNaturalist, Wikipedia taxoboxes) are not independent evidence. Your answer is shown beside the rules' records, never in place of them; "can't tell" costs nothing, so answer "can't tell" whenever what you find doesn't let you decide. Give a short reason for each answer.
